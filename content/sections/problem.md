The emotional cost of reporting is well known. There is also a *structural* barrier. Workplace harassment and online harassment or stalking share four properties of today's intake systems. Each one is well documented on its own. Together, they stop people from reporting.

| Structural barrier | What it costs the reporter |
|---|---|
| **Identity exposure.** A report attaches a name that HR, a trust & safety team, or eventually the accused may see. | The reporter alone carries the risk of retaliation (professional, social, sometimes physical), and carries it *before* anyone has made a decision. |
| **Repeated retraumatisation.** Every platform, form and investigator asks for the whole account again, usually from someone who cannot see its history. | The cost of reporting grows with each place where the abuse happened. |
| **The single-report dismissal.** One report can be dismissed as one person's word. A pattern cannot. | To find out whether they are the only one, a reporter must first expose themselves. The information that would justify the risk only arrives after taking it. |
| **Cross-context blindness.** A person who harasses a colleague at work and stalks a different woman online shows up as two unrelated incidents. | Harm that recurs across settings is the strongest evidence, and no single institution can see it. |
