These considerations are not an appendix. Several of them constrain the design.

- **Mandatory reporting.** In many jurisdictions, an intermediary has a legal duty to act once notified of certain conduct. A threshold notification can therefore start a process the reporters did not choose. Intake must say this in plain language before anyone files.
- **Consent is given per step and never carries over.** Filing a report is not consent to escalation. Escalation is not consent to identification. Being identified to one intermediary is not consent to being identified to another.
- **The threshold $k$ is a policy decision.** It trades the risk of a reporter going unheard against the risk of an unfounded investigation. It belongs to the deploying institution and its survivor-advocacy partner.
- **Due process for the accused.** A threshold event is a reason to investigate, never a finding. The system produces no evidence of wrongdoing, only evidence that distinct people filed reports.
- **Not a support service.** Every deployment must route reporters to human support. It must never present cryptographic guarantees as safety.

> **Research prototype.** SafeProof is not a support service, a legal instrument, or a substitute for trauma-informed care. It must not be used with real reporters without institutional review, legal counsel in the relevant jurisdiction, and a survivor-advocacy partner.
